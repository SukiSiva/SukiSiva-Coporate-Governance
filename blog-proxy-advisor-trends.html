Proxy Advisor Trends: Preparing for the 2027 AGM Season

Sir Alistair Thorne, KC & Jonathan Sterling June 25, 2026 6 Min Read Proxy Advisors, ISS, Glass Lewis, Executive Compensation, AGM

1. The Transatlantic Compliance Divergence

As multinational corporations finalize their 2027 statutory reporting strategies, corporate general counsels and audit committees find themselves caught between two fundamentally divergent regulatory philosophies. On one side of the Atlantic, the United States Securities and Exchange Commission (SEC) continues to focus squarely on traditional financial materialitymandating disclosures only when an issue possesses a substantial likelihood of impacting an investor's economic decision.

Conversely, the European Unions Corporate Sustainability Reporting Directive (CSRD), which now sweeps in thousands of non-EU parent entities with substantial European subsidiary revenue, strictly enforces the principle of double materiality.

Failing to reconcile European double-materiality metrics with US SEC filing statements creates catastrophic litigation exposure under SEC Rule 10b-5. What is disclosed in Brussels will inevitably be subpoenaed in Delaware.

2. Core Discrepancies: Financial Materiality vs. Double Materiality

Under the CSRD framework, an enterprise must evaluate sustainability impacts through two independent lenses:

  • Impact Materiality (Inside-Out): The positive or negative impact of the company's direct operations and upstream/downstream value chain on people, society, and the environment.
  • Financial Materiality (Outside-In): How external environmental and social factors trigger material financial risks, assets impairments, or liabilities for the enterprise.

Because information considered immaterial under SEC rules may be strictly mandatory under European CSRD standards, companies attempting to release a single unified global report risk either violating EU compliance mandates or inadvertently creating actionable admissions of liability under US securities law.

3. Director Duty of Care & Safe-Harbor Protections

The greatest risk for corporate directors lies in forward-looking statements. While the US Private Securities Litigation Reform Act (PSLRA) provides statutory safe-harbor protections for forward-looking statements accompanied by meaningful cautionary disclosures, the European regulatory regime does not extend equivalent blanket protections for voluntary aspirational targets.

When ambitious emissions reduction targets published in Europe fall short, US shareholder derivative plaintiff attorneys frequently use those published European disclosures as evidence of misleading corporate communications.

4. Practical Action Plan for Board Audit Committees

Governora recommends that corporate boards adopt the following three-tier harmonization framework before the Q1 2027 filing cycle:

  1. Establish Dual-Track Governance Pipelines: Segregate European subsidiary CSRD filings from parent company SEC Form 10-K disclosures, while maintaining joint legal verification of all quantitative data points.
  2. Third-Party Limited Assurance: Subject all material non-financial telemetry to independent auditor verification prior to cross-border filing submission.
  3. Board Oversight Minute Documentation: Explicitly record committee deliberations regarding materiality determinations to protect against personal director duty-of-oversight claims.
